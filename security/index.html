Security

← Back to DialectForge

Last updated: October 30, 2026

In short: FlowState keeps your project memory on your own computer. FlowStateMCP and FlowState Relay let cloud AI apps reach it without the relay being able to read your traffic. This page sums up how, what outside checks have found, and how to report a problem.

1. FlowState

  • FlowState is open source under the MIT license: github.com/dialectforge/FlowState.
  • Your project memory is one SQLite file on your own computer. FlowState needs no account.
  • Releases are tags signed with the FlowState release key, and flowstate update installs only a correctly signed release.

2. FlowStateMCP and FlowState Relay

  • The relay passes your encrypted connection through without opening it (TLS passthrough). Your TLS key is made on your computer and never leaves it.
  • Each AI app signs in to your own FlowState, and you approve it with your owner passphrase.
  • The full details, including the limits we know about, are on flowstatemcp.com/security.

3. Outside Checks

No outside firm has audited FlowState, the relay or flowstatemcp.com. A scan is not an audit. These are the outside checks done so far:

  • OAuth auth scan, October 2026: Auth-scanned by Leroy H. Mason using mcp-authscan, against his own beta relay address, with permission. Seven checks passed. The scan found that the token endpoint sent Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *. It also noted that /revoke refused standard RFC 7009 requests and that the OAuth issuer had a trailing slash. All three are fixed in FlowState 2.4.4.

4. Report a Vulnerability

Found a security problem in FlowState, FlowStateMCP or FlowState Relay? Email support@flowstatemcp.com. Tell us what you found and how to see it happen. Please don't send passwords, keys or tokens, and give us time to fix it before you make it public.

For other DialectForge products, contact info@dialectforge.com.
