Enterprise

The same seed-negotiated protocol, deployed across an organization. Corporate traffic is designed to be not just encrypted, but hard to identify.

§ 1 Pipeline
RefUse
E1On-site network protection

Run DF at the network edge so WiFi and wired traffic is encoded before it leaves the building. A tapped wire or captured WiFi frame is designed to show nothing identifiable.

E2Data in transit for regulated work

Healthcare, finance and legal teams get an extra layer on top of standard encryption. DF is meant to add to compliance work, not replace it.

E3Remote staff under DPI

Designed to let employees in countries with deep packet inspection reach company resources without the connection standing out as a VPN.

E4IoT & industrial control

SCADA, sensors and building systems talk through DF tunnels, so reconnaissance has a harder time even naming the protocols in use.

E5Multi-cloud mesh

Link AWS, Azure and GCP environments through a DF relay, without depending on each provider's peering.

E6Service-to-service

Microservices talk through DF tunnels, making lateral movement and architecture mapping harder for an intruder.

E7Branch & edge

Each site runs a container that tunnels back to HQ. No appliance to ship.

E8Organizational messaging

Double-DF messaging where the server routes sealed blobs and stores nothing. See Messenger.

Engineering notes

Dialect cycles can be tuned per network (30 s to 5 min). Dialect maps double as a compression layer: frequent patterns get short tokens during negotiation, with a design target of under 5% overhead. Frames injected into a stream are designed to fail validation, get dropped, and count toward anomaly detection. These are intended uses based on the protocol's design; none has been independently tested.
