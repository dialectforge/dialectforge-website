Licensing, in plain English

Everything is going open source. Here's what you can do with it, and when you'd want a commercial license instead.

§ 1 Status

Source code: coming soon

The repositories are being cleaned up and aren't public yet. When they open, they'll be announced on this site. Until then there's nothing to clone. Please don't go looking for unofficial copies.

§ 2 AGPL

What the AGPL-3.0 means for you

You can

  • Use it for anything, personal or business
  • Read, audit and test every line
  • Change it
  • Share it, changed or not
  • Charge for services or support around it

In return you must

  • Keep the license and copyright notices
  • Share your source if you hand out the software
  • Share your changes if you let people use a modified version over a network (that's the “Affero” part)
  • License your changes under the same terms

If you improve it and let other people use your improved version, you share the improvements. That keeps it open for everybody.

§ 3 Commercial

When you'd want a commercial license

If you're building a closed-source product, an appliance, or a hosted service and can't publish your changes, a commercial license from DialectForge LLC lets you use the AGPL code without those obligations. The same goes for enterprise deployments that need contract terms, support or custom work.

Email info@dialectforge.com and tell me what you're building. Nonprofits and community projects: ask anyway. Free licenses are possible.

Contributing

Once the repositories are public, contributions will need a signed contributor license agreement (CLA). That's what allows the dual licensing to work.

This page is a plain-language summary, not legal advice. The license files in each repository are what count.
