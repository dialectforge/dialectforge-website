AI Factory History

AI Factory was a proof I built to answer one question: can you give every piece of an AI's knowledge its own seed and then revoke one piece cleanly? It did what it was built for. The work moved on to Lu and Cattledog.

§ 1 What it proved

Revocation works

In AI Factory, every fact was its own unit with its own DialectForge seed, and facts were joined by bonds. Revoking a seed cut off that knowledge and what was derived from it, and left a scar: a record of what was removed and why. The demo below shows that working in the AI Factory terminal. That was the point of the project: to show revocation and scars can work in practice, not just on paper.

It was a proof of concept, built and tested by me, not independently reviewed. The code is being kept for reference and will be published under AGPL-3.0 with the other DialectForge projects.

A test joint. You cut it to see if the idea holds before you build the real thing.

§ 2 What came next

Lu

The ideas from AI Factory grew into Lu, an AI built on a knowledge graph rather than on model weights. Knowledge is stored as typed pieces joined by weighted, typed bonds, and it thinks by spreading activation through them, so an answer can be traced back to what produced it. It runs continuously with a curiosity engine, says “I don't know” instead of making things up, and uses scars to keep removed knowledge from coming back. It has also hit real limits with training, memory (RAM) and brain size. It's now open source under AGPL-3.0 (github.com/dialectforge/lu), and Cattledog came out of it. Full details: the Lu page.

§ 3 Cattledog
Open source · Apache-2.0

Cattledog

Cattledog takes Lu's honesty and revocation (“scars”) mechanisms and wraps them around an outside LLM. A cattle dog doesn't own the herd; it keeps it moving the right way. It has its own repository under the permissive Apache-2.0 license: github.com/dialectforge/cattledog. Full details: the Cattledog page.

  1. Gate what the model may do

    The design maps a model's tool requests to capability seeds and checks them against revocations and scars, aiming to keep a revoked capability blocked however the request is worded.

  2. Check the answer (“off-ness”)

    After the model answers, the answer is compared with grounded knowledge. If it looks “off”, it's sent back to be asked again a capped number of times, and if it still can't be grounded the answer becomes “I don't know”. This part is implemented but switched off by default, and so far it's only been run against a test fixture, not real data.

Nothing in Cattledog has been independently reviewed. Don't treat it as a guarantee against prompt injection or made-up answers. AI agent prompt for Cattledog.

The AI can bark all it wants. The dog decides which gates open, and it double-checks the work.
