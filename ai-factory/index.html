AI Factory, Lu & Cattledog

What happens when the protocol isn't just guarding an AI but is how the AI's memory is built. And how that same idea can keep an outside AI model in line.

§ 1 AI Factory → Lu

Knowledge you can remove one piece at a time

Today's AI models smear what they know across billions of weights. You can't fix one wrong fact without retraining. AI Factory, which has grown into a system I call Lu, takes a different approach. Every fact is its own unit with its own DialectForge seed, and facts are joined by typed bonds. Recall works by spreading activation through those bonds.

1.1Discrete knowledge

Every fact can be addressed on its own: looked up, corrected or removed.

1.2Surgical revocation & scar memory

Revoke a seed and that knowledge, plus everything derived from it, is cut off. It leaves a scar recording what was removed and why, which resists the same connection forming again.

1.3Terminal bond

The AI bonds with its operator through the protocol. Only commands through that bond are treated as commands; everything else is data to look at.

1.4Transparent reasoning & an honesty floor

Answers come with the path through memory that produced them. When the memory doesn't have it, the design is to say “I don't know” rather than make something up.

1.5Designed to resist prompt injection

The goal isn't to suppress injected instructions, it's that they're never recognized as instructions in the first place.

Instead of one big blur of knowledge, it's a shop full of labeled drawers. If one drawer is wrong, you pull that drawer and leave a note saying why.

§ 2 Cattledog
Coming open source · Apache-2.0

Cattledog: a safety layer that herds an outside model

A cattle dog doesn't own the herd. It keeps it moving the right way. Cattledog sits between an untrusted AI model and the things it's allowed to touch:

  1. Gate the tool calls

    When the model asks to use a tool, the request is mapped to a capability seed and checked against revocations and scars. It's designed to be wording-invariant: a revoked capability stays blocked however the prompt is phrased.

  2. Check the answer

    After the model answers, Cattledog checks the answer against its own grounded memory. If the answer is “off,” it sends it back and asks again, a capped number of times, and if it still can't ground it, it says “I don't know.”

It was proven out in a sandbox with a small open model, graded from logs of what tools actually ran rather than by another AI. It's early. It'll be released under the permissive Apache-2.0 license so AI teams can use it anywhere.

The AI can bark all it wants, but it only goes through the gates the dog opens, and the dog double-checks its work.
